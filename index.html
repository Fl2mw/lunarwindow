Why is the lunar south pole so hard?

☀️ The Sun skims the horizonThe Moon’s axis is tilted only 1.5°, so at the pole the Sun circles the sky and never climbs more than ~1.5° up. A small hill can cause darkness that lasts for days.
🌍 Earth bobs up and downEarth stays in nearly the same spot in the sky, but libration (the Moon’s wobble) moves it ±7° over a month. Near the pole that’s enough to put Earth below the horizon, and then there’s no direct radio link.
⛰️ Higher ground winsWith no air, the horizon is just terrain plus the curve of a small sphere. A lander 100 m up a ridge sees ~0.6° further down, which often decides whether it gets sunlight at all.
📅 A delay changes everythingA lunar day is 29.5 Earth days. Slip the launch a week and you might land at dusk instead of dawn. That’s why rapid date and site comparison matters.
